# Assembly

Assembly follows the reverse order of disassembly but must be documented as an explicit procedure rather than relying on “reverse the previous steps.” Include cable and hose routing, connector checks, lubrication, fastener replacement requirements, tightening sequence, torque, adjustment, and a final functional test.

Use `MON-###` identifiers for assembly procedures. Link every step to the relevant [fastener](../reference/fasteners.md), [torque](../reference/torque-specifications.md), [tool](../reference/tools.md), and [connector](../reference/connectors.md) records.

## Front hub motor axle

The front-wheel axle is also the hub motor axle. A shaped feature on the axle acts as a mechanical anti-rotation feature: when correctly seated in the corresponding fork dropout, it prevents the axle from rotating relative to the fork while the motor drives the wheel. A transcript reference to a retaining tab describes this axle feature, **not a tab on a washer**. The front axle washers observed during the repair were completely round.

{% hint style="danger" %}
When installing the front hub motor, fully seat the axle's anti-rotation feature in the matching fork dropout before tightening the axle nuts. An incorrectly seated axle can rotate relative to the fork under motor torque.
{% endhint %}

Keep every washer in its recorded position and order. The number, dimensions, material, exact installation order, axle and dropout geometry, and applicability of the recorded [front axle nut torque](../reference/torque-specifications.md#manufacturer-specified-torques) to the observed configuration must still be verified.
