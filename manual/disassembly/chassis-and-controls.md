# DEM-004: Chassis, controls, and electronics

## Kick Lock

Use Security Torx T25, not T30, on the observed countersunk fastener (estimated 12–15 mm; exact thread and torque unknown). Blue threadlocker made the locking bolt particularly difficult to break free; after one side released, the bolt pressed out readily. The bolt appeared stainless and has an unidentified black centre insert.

The lock was heavily sealed. Its fault remained internal: the pin could be pressed in and then pushed out again with almost no resistance. A spring or electromagnetic fault was considered but not confirmed. Do not diagnose from this symptom alone.

## Fenders and brakes

Rear-fender fasteners have an external hex and Phillips recess. Retain the plastic spacers at the seat-stay bridge and forward mount in their original positions. The front fender uses similar fasteners and blue threadlocker. Its two stay set screws appeared to take 1.5 mm hex (2 mm was too large), but both were seized and one socket was already rounded; stop before worsening a damaged socket and plan controlled extraction.

Corrosion was observed low on the fork near the front axle. Establish its depth before refinishing or returning the fork to service.

The rear pads had an estimated 1.5–2 mm of friction material and uneven, angled wear. The front pads had approximately 3–4 mm (about 3.5 mm) and appeared good; their uncertain marking was `2291`. These estimates are not wear limits. Find the cause of angled wear, inspect the rotor, and use the brake maker's actual minimum thickness.

Both caliper-bolt tool sockets were damaged; replace the bolts with correctly specified parts rather than assuming stainless is suitable. Caliper markings observed for future identification are front `J19 8380` and rear `J19 7901`.

## Handlebar, levers, buttons, and internal routing

1. Remove the rubber plug from the front underside of the handlebar.
2. With a small hook or pliers, gently draw both horizontal cables down through the opening and clear of the steerer.
3. Remove both handlebar clamp bolts with a 5 mm hex key. They were measured as M6 × 28 mm; installation torque is 7 Nm.
4. Lift off the handlebar and capture every spacer in order.

{% hint style="danger" %}
Cables left across the steerer space can be crushed when the bar is installed, especially with no spacers. Pull them clear before removal and installation, and verify free, unpinched routing before tightening.
{% endhint %}

The left and right grips are not interchangeable generic grips: at least one has a moulded or machined button-cable channel. Both workshop grips were dirty and damaged. The bell and boost buttons are bedded in silicone (not hot glue) and retained by small set screws; the right screw was missing. A 2 mm hex fitted one 4–5 mm set screw. One side used electrical tape, while the apparently original side used thick industrial double-sided tape over the cable. Removing a button required removing tape and silicone and desoldering its lead. The boost button separated into two pieces, so inspect it before reuse.

Remove the internal plastic cover over the brake hoses and harness before trying to pull the right brake line upward; a strong light and inverting the frame improved access. A rain seal was removed from the right hose for movement. Secure all short leads before moving the fork—the Kick Lock plug was lost inside the frame during the logged work.

## Front light

The underside trim is positioned by two plastic pins and silicone. The light is retained by an M5 × 8 mm security-hex set screw; a T10 bit released the documented screw only as an improvised fit and is not the correct tool. Label and secure the electrical lead during removal. Verify the lamp marking and connector before ordering a replacement.

## Smart Cartridge and antenna cover

Use a confirmed Torx T9 bit for Cartridge removal. Keep the fasteners separated: short countersunk screws belong to the Smart Cartridge; long flat-head screws belong to the antenna cover. The fastener register records eight M3 × 8 mm Cartridge/socket screws and two M2.5 × 6 mm antenna-cover screws, but reconcile those measurements with the particular bicycle during removal.

The rear Cartridge screw under the saddle was exceptionally awkward. A frustrated workshop note proposed omitting it; **do not do so**. Treat it as required until VanMoof documentation proves otherwise.

## Harness handling

The documented internal harness was broken and a professional replacement estimate of about €800 was mentioned historically; this is neither a current price nor a diagnosis. Before pulling an internal harness, attach a durable draw wire so a replacement can follow the same path. Tape connectors to the draw wire without making a shoulder that can snag, protect paint from adhesive, and label every branch. The internal guide also carries two brake hoses and shares the battery/bottom-bracket region, although the exact routing still needs mapping.
