# DEM-003: Drivetrain and bottom bracket

This chapter consolidates observations from the workshop logs. **Verified** means observed on the documented S3; dimensions described as estimates still require measurement.

## Chain, chain cover, and tensioner

1. Remove the rear wheel. The chain remains captive around the frame until its directional master link is opened. Record the link's running direction before opening it; its exact make and reusability are unknown.
2. Remove the rear chain-cover screw with a 2.5 mm hex key. The short screw has a washer. After the right crank is removed, the main chain cover pulls off without tools; it is clipped on rather than additionally screwed in.
3. Remove the tensioner mounting with a 4 mm hex key. A documented housing fastener is M4 × 18 mm FHCS (3 mm hex), and a second is approximately M4 × 24 mm BHCS (3 mm hex). A 2.5 mm hex fastener retains the reinforcing clip.
4. If opening the tensioner, contain it before separating the housing: a preloaded torsion spring can eject the mechanism and small stop screw. One wheel is retained by an FHCS and nut; the long rear fastener uses a washer and self-locking nut. Photograph the stack before separation.

{% hint style="danger" %}
Do not reuse the documented tensioner without inspecting it. Its mounting eyes were broken and the housing had multiple microcracks. The load is concentrated at an eye that also closes the housing.
{% endhint %}

The two moulded-plastic jockey wheels use press-fit bearings marked `689 RS` (the complete marking still needs confirmation). They and their washers were heavily contaminated. See [maintenance](../maintenance/README.md#chain-and-chain-tensioner).

## Pedals

The official workshop reference specifies a 6 mm internal hex, opposite thread directions (right pedal: normal right-hand thread; left pedal: left-hand thread), and 40 Nm installation torque. A 15 mm pedal wrench also removed the right pedal in practice. Penetrating oil was needed on a seized pedal. Markings observed were `CM-R0H2` on the right and an uncertain `CM-L/OH2` on the left.

Do not confuse an earlier log's 8 mm tool observation with the pedal spindle: 8 mm is the crank-bolt drive. Clean threads and confirm whether the observed blue threadlocker and sealing washer were original before reassembly.

## Square-taper cranks

Tools: 8 mm hex key, square-taper crank puller (a Fengzeo puller was proven in practice), and 16 mm open-end wrench.

1. Remove the M8 × 20 mm central crank bolt and rubber sealing ring with an 8 mm hex key. Blue threadlocker and slight thread corrosion were observed.
2. Thread the puller's outer body fully and squarely into the crank's extractor thread. The suspected `M22 × 1` extractor thread has not been measured.
3. Turn the pressure spindle against the bottom-bracket axle with a 16 mm wrench. Hold the crank as the counter-support; a roughly 30° wrench-to-crank angle allows controlled opposing hand pressure.
4. Expect substantial but progressive force from the taper fit. The arm moves off continuously rather than springing free. Repeat on the other side.

{% hint style="danger" %}
Never lever against the plastic bottom-bracket parts or hammer the crank off. A workshop attempt damaged the plastic closure. Engage the puller fully or its limited thread engagement can strip the crank.
{% endhint %}

The inexpensive puller's soft pressure tip showed wear after two cranks. Prefer its broad support adaptor if it fits, so the tip does not point-load the axle bore.

Both forged crank arms are marked `VanMoof`, `170 mm`, and `GJ-9AS`. The replaceable chainring has 38 teeth and five hex-socket chainring bolts with flat rear counter-holds. On assembly, place the arms 180° apart and draw them onto the clean taper only with their bolts. A cap appeared to state 38–42 Nm, but that value remains unverified and must not be treated as a specification.

## Bafang bottom bracket

A Shimano-style tool tested in the workshop did not fit. The assembly comprises a right drive-side protective cup, a left keyed support with anti-rotation tabs, the bearing/sensor unit, and seals.

Correct removal order:

1. Remove the **right protective cup clockwise**; it has a left-hand thread.
2. Push the complete unit a few millimetres from left to right until the left support's tabs leave their frame guides.
3. Only then unscrew the left support normally (counter-clockwise; right-hand thread).
4. Withdraw the complete bottom bracket and protect its cable and connector.

{% hint style="danger" %}
Do not force the left support while its anti-rotation tabs are loaded. A cracking noise during the first attempt was attributed to those tabs, not a seized thread. Move the unit first to unload them and avoid damaging the support or harness.
{% endhint %}

The sensor has slight axial play that appeared constructional rather than defective. Its lead ends in a waterproof six-pole Higo connector with a white dot and `03` marking. Secure this and every short connector against falling into the frame.

The right rubber seal was cracked and should be replaced; the cleaned left seal appeared reusable. Both can be carefully lifted from their cups with a small screwdriver. They may be standard Bafang parts, but that identification is not confirmed. The bottom-bracket shell contains a frame drain opening; keep it clear.
